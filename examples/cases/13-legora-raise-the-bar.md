#### Case 13: Legora — Raise the Bar

**What this case teaches:** A familiar word can connect an audience, a brand benefit and an experience. Follow its different meanings far enough to discover what people could actually do.

**Status:** Nicolas Chan supplied the final proposal and reported the pitch win on 2 October 2026. This is a retrospective reconstruction of its logic, not a record of how the authors discovered the idea or evidence that AI generated it. Campaign delivery, client selection reasons and performance results were not supplied. All activities below are proposed. Sources S1–S3 are identified at the end.

### The connection that matters

**Lawyers → the Bar / bar exam → raise the bar → higher standards → a literal bar activation.**

| Path | Meaning it contributes |
|---|---|
| Legal profession → the Bar | A connection to the audience's world. |
| Better standards → raise the bar | The benefit Legora wants people to expect from AI built for legal work. |
| Bar → a place to gather over drinks | A physical setting for the campaign and a reason to come together. |

The phrase helps generate the activation. The activation makes another meaning of the phrase visible. That is why this is more than a legal pun added to an event.

Chan identified another possible path: **lawyers → a stereotype about enjoying drinking → drinks bar**. This is a cultural association supplied during the review, not research about lawyers' drinking habits. It illustrates how a second path can reach the same word; it does not independently verify the audience fit. The platform does not depend on that stereotype. (S2)

The legal association and the standards idiom need not share an etymology. Their present meanings are enough to explore the connection. Nor must every execution take place in a bar: upgrading a familiar coffee or lunch can express the same invitation to expect better.

### The nine-step reconstruction

**Step 1 — Zeitgeist**
The proposal starts with lawyers already using a mixture of general-purpose and specialist AI, acquired through familiarity, convenience or firm procurement. This is the proposal's diagnosis, not a market-wide adoption finding. (S1, slides 7–8)

**Step 2 — Tension**
Lawyers want useful assistance, but a new tool asks them to learn another interface and change familiar habits. An existing arrangement can retain value even when it has limitations. (S1, slides 8–9)

**Step 3 — Insight**
**Knowing how to make an existing tool work can make an adequate default feel more valuable than an unfamiliar improvement.** This is a hypothesis reconstructed from the proposal: changing has an immediate effort cost, while the benefit is still uncertain. It requires audience validation. (S1, slides 8–9; retrospective interpretation)

**Step 4 — Role**
Legora invites lawyers to expect more from their tools: assistance with research, review and drafting that leaves more room for judgement and advice. Higher standards are the intended brand promise; the proposal does not establish comparative product superiority. (S1, slides 10–12)

**Step 5 — Call to Action**
Reconsider the usual. Take a first look at Legora, then investigate its relevance to real legal work and possible use with the team. The immediate activation asks for a QR visit; it does not require an immediate software purchase. (S1, slides 13–14)

**Step 6 — Strategy / GWTB**

```text
GET legal professionals already comfortable with their current tools
FROM accepting the familiar arrangement without reconsidering it
TO taking a fresh look at what Legora could add to their legal work
BY interrupting "the usual" with an appealing upgrade, then connecting
   that invitation to relevant product demonstrations and practitioner discussion.
```

This is an analytical restatement of slides 7–14, not a verbatim strategy supplied by the client. The everyday analogy is ordering the usual: both choices can continue through familiarity without being reassessed.

**Step 7 — Creative Platform**
**Raise the Bar.** Expect a higher standard from the tools supporting legal work. The legal meaning supplies relevance, the idiom supplies the promise, and the venue meaning supplies an experience. (S1, slides 11–14; S2)

**Step 8 — Big Idea**
Interrupt the routine of ordering the usual at coffee, lunch and after-work venues. People encounter the invitation, scan a QR code to Legora's website and receive an upgraded coffee, sandwich or cocktail. The intended connection is simple: a familiar choice can be improved. The proposed drinks-bar gathering adds networking and product demonstrations. Venue takeovers and a single pop-up residency are alternative formats, not confirmed bookings. (S1, slides 14–16)

A better drink is an illustration, not evidence of better legal output or easier software migration. The product encounter still has to earn that conclusion.

**Step 9 — Small Ideas**

- **Raise & Shine:** a media breakfast and practitioner discussion about workload, legal standards and preserving human judgement. Singapore breakfast favourites receive Swedish-inspired upgrades, extending the campaign's rule. (S1, slide 17)
- **Editorial stories:** give journalists access to people and evidence about what specialist AI adds, how it works on a real matter and what lawyers should gain from time saved. These are proposed stories, not secured coverage. (S1, slides 18–19)
- **Workflow mockumentaries:** one proposed episode shows a lawyer who has highlighted almost an entire contract. Assistance helps her identify points for review; she checks the relevant passages herself. Workplace comedy leads into a specific product use. (S1, slides 20–21)
- **Professional engagement and distribution:** association invitations, discussion events and paid/social amplification connect people to the activation and subsequent consideration. Outdoor placements are optional support. (S1, slides 22–25)

### What the earlier routes had — and what they missed

| Earlier route | Useful connection | Difference from Raise the Bar |
|---|---|---|
| **No. Law.** | Singlish lor/law soundplay connects routine document tasks to professional identity. | The joke creates recognition; the final phrase also generates a promise and a setting people can enter. |
| **The Overqualified Games** | Office skills become sporting events, including the Track Changes Relay. | It dramatises specific workflows. Raise the Bar holds a broader brand invitation, with workflow comedy as one supporting strand. |
| **Put Legora on the Stand** | Legal scrutiny becomes a live opportunity to question the brand. | It already turns language into participation. Raise the Bar instead begins with an everyday routine and an attractive upgrade. Whether that attracts more participation remains untested. |

These were earlier tissue-test routes, not losing campaigns with measured results. Their differences help explain the creative development; they do not prove the winner would outperform them. (S3)

The recorded work concentrated on developing judgement, admin, tables, sporting metaphors and the stand. It did not show a developed batch of broader legal-word association chains, even though Chain and Unfold were available. The missed opportunity was to explore more connections before narrowing. More versions of the same headline would not have supplied that breadth. (S2–S3)

### How to use the lesson

One suitable combination is [W04 Chain and W10 Unfold](../../method/research-operators.md), followed by [A18 Take It Literally](../../catalog/A-language-and-word-craft.md):

1. **Chain:** keep an obvious seed such as the Bar, then branch into its different senses. Also start from the benefit, higher standards, and notice where the paths meet.
2. **Unfold:** retrieve the established expression raise the bar. Ask what each reading contributes rather than counting meanings for their own sake.
3. **Take It Literally:** make the venue meaning an experience. Describe the invitation, action, immediate reward and connection to Legora.

The [four development checks](../../method/creative-operator-routing.md#generate-and-compare-before-polishing) make the selection legible:

- **Natural connections:** the legal association, familiar idiom and venue meaning can each be explained plainly.
- **Useful brand meaning:** higher standards link to the intended benefit of assistance built for legal work.
- **Something to see or do:** people can attend, upgrade an order, talk and explore the product.
- **More than one execution:** hospitality, a media discussion and workplace films have different jobs under the same invitation.

These are reasons to develop and test the candidate. They are not audience validation. The familiar phrase is not exclusive to Legora; consistent brand attribution and credible product evidence still matter.

Use this case to practise finding and developing connections alongside the rest of the catalogue. Do not require every brief to produce a pun, a bar or this technique sequence. This known winner is a teaching reconstruction, not a fresh discovery test.
