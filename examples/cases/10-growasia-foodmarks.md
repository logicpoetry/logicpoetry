#### Case 10: GrowAsia / FoodMarks (NGO / Food Security / Southeast Asia)

**Zeitgeist**: Food is core to Southeast Asian identity. It is culture, memory, family, and pride. Ask any Southeast Asian what defines their heritage and food is in the answer within seconds.

**Tension**: That same food identity is eroding — slowly, quietly — as the farming systems that sustain it face climate change, aging farmer populations, and unsustainable practices. Consumers and corporate funders understand the problem abstractly. But there is no urgency. The rice bowl is still full today.

**Insight**: Modern brands spend millions building corporate identity — naming rights for arenas, brand colors that consumers recognize on sight, partnerships that extend the brand into new cultural territory. What if *food* could be part of a brand's corporate identity? If a company's name could be on a farm plot, on produce packaging, on a dish — the food security conversation stops being abstract. It becomes a brand asset. Companies don't need to be altruistic. They need a reason to CARE and a way to BENEFIT.

**Role**: GrowAsia as the platform that connects brands' ESG ambitions to Southeast Asian farmers — with visible branding returns at every stage.

**Creative Platform**: "FoodMarks" — *Like a trademark, but for food security*

**GWTB**:
```
WHO form:
GET public and private sector leaders in Southeast Asia
WHO have ESG commitments but no compelling reason to direct them toward food security specifically
TO claim a FoodMark as a visible part of their corporate identity — from farm to table
BY giving them branding returns at every stage of the supply chain

FROM form:
GET organisations with CSR/ESG budgets
FROM treating food security as a generic charitable cause with no brand return
TO making a FoodMark — a committed, visible, branded stake in Southeast Asian food systems
BY creating touchpoints across the supply chain where the investment shows up publicly
```

**Big Idea**: Like a trademark, a "FoodMark" is a branded ESG commitment that earns companies visibility across the food supply chain:
- **From farm**: "Adopt" a plot of land (updates on FoodMarked crops; press release announcing the adoption/partnership)
- **To commerce**: Co-branding on produce packaging in supermarkets (QR code linking to brand's role in bringing farm to table; social media contest around FoodMarked produce)
- **To kitchens**: Design a unique corporate dish in partnership with a local celebrity chef (media tasting event; second press release; employee LinkedIn advocacy)

**News hook**: Campaign launch timed to World Food Day (October 16) — gives media a pre-built editorial reason to cover it.

**Amplification Roadmap**:
- Press release on World Food Day announcing adoption/partnership + joint interviews with brand partner (e.g. DBS) and GrowAsia
- FoodMarked produce launch at selected grocery stores with packaging going live
- Social media contest for brand partner (quiz around FoodMarked produce; winners receive free produce)
- Employee advocacy programme for brand partner — LinkedIn posts/reposts creating FOMO among other corporate partners
- Celebrity chef co-creation launch: separate press release + media tasting event

**Why It Works** (2 criteria):
1. ESG is increasingly core to brands' corporate identity — FoodMarks gives organisations a reason to CARE that maps to something they already invest in
2. B2B2C brand awareness potential — every FoodMark creates consumer-facing touchpoints that extend the brand partner's identity in ways standard CSR donations cannot

**Budget / Timeline**: ~SGD 64K | July–February (8 months)

**Creative Moves in play**: ESG-as-brand-asset; supply chain as narrative arc; news hook timing (World Food Day)
