#### Case 11: Fandom (B2B Tech / China Market Entry)

**Zeitgeist**: For businesses in cultural export — games, entertainment, IP-based brands — the next leap comes from building brand and IP, not just acquiring users. League of Legends creates a pop group (K/DA). Honor of Kings spins off a TV drama. Players join for the gameplay; they stay for the story and the characters.

**Tension**: In China, Fandom's competitors all talk the same language — more users, more data, bigger numbers. The category is a metrics arms race. Fandom needs a differentiated entry that cuts through without out-spending incumbents.

**Insight**: Fandom has built the world's largest collection of entertainment lore. It literally knows how great stories are built — what makes IP sticky, what makes characters live beyond their source material, what makes fans *inhabit* a world rather than just consume a product. No competitor can credibly claim that. The authoritative POV on *why* some IPs go big is Fandom's and Fandom's alone.

**Role**: Go-to world-building partner for brands that want to build great IPs; go-to platform for fans who want to live inside those worlds.

**Creative Platform**: "The Next Great Story Is Yours" / 缔造下一个传奇

**GWTB**:
```
WHO form:
GET cultural export brands in China wanting to take their IP global
WHO are starting to hit the ceiling of pure user acquisition and product marketing
TO see brand marketing and IP equity-building as the next phase of growth
BY positioning Fandom as the authoritative partner in world-building — and the platform where their fans already live

FROM form:
GET cultural export brands
FROM focusing on user acquisition and product marketing (diminishing returns at scale)
TO brand marketing and IP equity-building through compelling characters and stories
BY being the go-to partner for world-building and the go-to platform for fans who care about the brand's fandom
```

**Big Idea**: "The Next Great Story Is Yours" Going Global Summit — a Fandom-hosted event in China putting the spotlight on the craft of IP-building. The point of difference isn't the speakers — it's the *type* of speaker. Instead of "Heads of Marketing," the summit features legendary authors, creative directors, story directors, prop masters, and script writers. People who actually build worlds for a living. Fandom deconstructs what makes great stories great — and in doing so, demonstrates why Fandom's products are indispensable.

**Small Ideas**:
- **"Inside The Director's Chair"**: 12 months × 12 stories behind great IPs × 12 luminaries from the biggest franchises — WeChat content series establishing Fandom as the editorial home for IP craft
- **"Greatest Fandoms of the Year Almanac"**: Annual data report powered by Fandom.com, revealing the year's biggest IP movements according to real fan behaviour. Launched with a media event (salon format) + downloadable report. Creates an owned annual media moment.
- **Fandom WeChat Launch**: Official WeChat service account — online hub for all things storytelling; upgraded to SCRM (social CRM) for lead generation as traction builds
- **PR Storybank**: Always-on evergreen pitch angles — stories Fandom can supply to media year-round without needing a new campaign hook

**Campaign Roadmap**:
- *Awareness* (Laying the Foundation): WeChat launch + local story bank; introducing Fandom's vision to China market
- *Interest/Consideration* (Building Interest): Going Global Summit + Q&As with creative directors
- *Conversion* (Amplifying the Story): "Fans' Selection" index launch + digital and social amplification

**Why It Works** (3 criteria):
1. Unique POV — competitors talk metrics for years; Fandom talks craft. Different conversation entirely.
2. KOL type-flip — story directors and authors instead of marketing heads. Same credibility tier; completely different (and more compelling) voice.
3. Narrative organic to brand — Fandom built its entire existence on breaking down great stories. The summit is the product doing what the product does.

**Creative Moves in play**: KOL type-flip; product-as-proof; B2B thought leadership as the Big Idea; data product as always-on credibility anchor
