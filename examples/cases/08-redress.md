#### Case 8: Redress (Sustainability / Fashion / HK)

**Zeitgeist**: Hong Kongers are fast-fashion buyers. "FAST is in our DNA." Speed is a cultural value — in business, in food, in fashion. Staying current is a form of identity.

**Tension**: Fast fashion is irresistible but socially irresponsible. Everyone knows it. Nobody wants to be lectured about it. The challenge isn't awareness — it's making preowned *desirable*, not just defensible.

**Insight**: Being fashionably relevant AND socially relevant are not mutually exclusive. The barrier to preowned isn't values — it's perception. People assume preowned = less stylish. The insight is that assumption is wrong, and if you can prove it before they know what they're wearing, the assumption collapses.

**Strategy**: Curate UNEXPECTED fashionable experiences with preowned garments. Let people fall in love with the look before they know the story.

**Creative Platform**: #SecondChanceFashionista

**GWTB**:
```
WHO form:
GET trend-conscious Hong Kongers
WHO assume preowned garments can't compete with fast fashion on style
TO experience preowned as fashionably exciting — not just ethically correct
BY letting them fall in love with the look before the reveal

FROM form:
GET fast fashion buyers
FROM dismissing preowned as a compromise
TO choosing preowned as a first option
BY proving they can't tell the difference — and then telling them
```

**Big Idea**: An interactive pop-up inside a Tier 1 mall (surrounded by Zara, H&M, and other fast fashion anchors). Four steps:
1. Visitors use a digital screen to curate a fashionable look from The Redress Closet — mixing and matching garments. The brand and preowned nature are NOT revealed.
2. Try on the actual garments in the pop-up changing room.
3. The reveal: "#SecondChanceFashionista" — these garments are all preowned.
4. Happy with the look? Share your OOTD on social media with the hashtag to get the outfit free.

**Campaign Roadmap**:
- **Teaser**: Macro fashion influencers seed trendy preowned looks as OOTD content — no brand mention. CTA hints at a "mix & match experience" at a selected mall.
- **Launch**: Pop-up opens. Media invited as a "photo hotspot." Brand only revealed after they participate. Thank-you card with key messages given to every visitor. Media advised to hold the brand name in early coverage to preserve the surprise.
- **Impact Reveal**: Press release recapping activation — number of looks created, volume of textile waste avoided. The data proves the campaign's own thesis.
- **Stylist-Recreated Look**: Eco-conscious stylists and micro next-gen influencers recreate the #SecondChanceFashionista looks. CTA links to Redress's Takeback programme donation boxes.

**Why It Works** (3 criteria):
1. Simple attitude shift — not asking for lifestyle overhaul, just one different decision
2. Gradual discovery — lets consumers choose preowned rather than be pushed into it
3. Appeals to the desire to be socially relevant, not just fashionably relevant

**Budget / Timeline**: ~HKD 200K | May–July (4 months)

**Budget note**: Pro-bono agency fees + barter trade with influencers and stylist + free Tier 1 mall venue = most of the budget goes to production. Strong model for NGO campaigns with brand partners.

**Creative Moves in play**: Reveal mechanic (eliminate prejudice before the hook); partnership-as-budget
