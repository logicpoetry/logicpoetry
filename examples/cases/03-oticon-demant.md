#### Case 3: Oticon / Demant (Healthcare / Hearing Aids)

**Zeitgeist**: Hearing loss is framed as a patient problem. The conversation is always about the person who can't hear — their diagnosis, their treatment, their hearing aid.

**Tension**: We assume the patient is the only one affected. But we're looking at the wrong person.

**Insight**: When a parent loses their hearing, the entire communication dynamic of a family reverses. Children become the explainers. Parents become the ones who need to be heard — not the ones who listen. The relationship that was always one direction (parent listens, guides, responds) quietly breaks. The family can no longer *listen* to the parent. The loop closes only when the parent can hear again.

**Subject Flip**: Patient → Caregiver becomes the protagonist. The entire campaign is written from the adult child's point of view.

**Creative Platform**: Listen Again

**GWTB**:
```
WHO form:
GET adult children of aging parents
WHO feel a creeping distance from someone they used to be close to
TO recognize hearing loss as the invisible wall — and Oticon as the door
BY showing that giving your parent the ability to hear is giving yourself the ability to listen

FROM form:
GET adult children of aging parents
FROM feeling helpless as the relationship with their parent quietly fractures
TO taking action to restore the connection they've been missing
BY showing that Oticon doesn't just help parents hear — it lets families listen again
```

**Manifesto** (Register B — Emotional/Human):
The communication lifecycle of a parent and child: first words → first arguments → first big life conversations → adulthood, when the child calls home not because they have to, but because they want to. And then — gradually — the calls get harder. Not because there's less to say. Because one of you can no longer hear. "We long for them to hear us, so that we in return, can listen." Oticon. Listen Again.

**Big Idea**: Hero video in local market, directed by local creators, following an adult child preparing to have a real conversation with an aging parent — only possible now because of hearing restoration.

**Small Ideas**:
- App experience: Oticon Online Hearing Test — low-friction first step for adult children to refer a parent
- "WhatsApp University" campaign: chain-message format with real hearing loss statistics, designed natively for the way middle-older gen consumes information. Shareable, source-light, emotionally pitched to the adult child sending it to siblings.
- Media panel: audiologists + adult children of hearing-loss patients as story sources
