### CANNES GRAND PRIX CALIBRATION — 2023 to 2026

Eight Grand Prix winners read through the chain. Use them to check the altitude of a Tension, an Insight or a Big Idea against work that won at the top of the industry. Each entry names the step it calibrates best. Award facts were checked against festival and trade coverage in October 2026; the original working draft had dated the set 2023 to 2025 and that span was wrong.

---

#### AXA — "Three Words" (Publicis Conseil, Paris · Creative Effectiveness Grand Prix 2026)

- **Calibrates**: Tension, Role.
- **Tension**: home insurance covered a house made uninhabitable by fire or flood, but not one made uninhabitable by domestic violence.
- **Role**: the insurer adds three words to its policy so that leaving an abuser is covered like any other emergency.
- **Why it wins**: the product itself changes. The idea is a clause, not a film. The brand's superpower is the only thing that could resolve the tension.
- **Source type**: editorial mining. The idea came from reading the policy against the lived reality of its customers.

#### Orange — "WoMen's Football" (Marcel, Paris · Entertainment Lions for Sport Grand Prix 2024)

- **Calibrates**: Insight, Creative.
- **Insight**: we do not lack respect for the skill, we lack respect for the person. Show the same skill on a man's body and the room applauds.
- **Creative mechanic**: expected visual first, then the reveal that the highlights were the women's team all along.
- **Why it wins**: the audience catches its own bias in real time. Nobody is lectured.
- **Operation**: Bait-and-Switch (below).

#### Specsavers — "The Misheard Version" (Golin, London · two Grand Prix 2024, including Audio & Radio)

- **Calibrates**: Call to Action, Small Ideas.
- **Insight**: a hearing test should feel like a joke you are in on, not a medical chore.
- **Creative mechanic**: Rick Astley re-records "Never Gonna Give You Up" with the lyrics people mishear. The song is the hearing test.
- **Why it wins**: a heavy health message rides inside a meme the culture already loves. Bookings rose because the ask was fun to say yes to.
- **Operation**: Cultural Trojan Horse (below).

#### Xbox — "The Everyday Tactician" (McCann, London · Direct Grand Prix 2024; Creative Effectiveness Gold 2025)

- **Calibrates**: Big Idea.
- **Big Idea**: hire a Football Manager player as a full-time analyst at Bromley FC and film what happens to the real club.
- **Why it wins**: the proof is an act, not a claim. The game's promise (you could run a club) is tested in public with real stakes.
- **Operation**: Build a Utility (below). The Big Idea is a job, not an ad.

#### Penny — "Price Packs" (Serviceplan, Munich · Print & Publishing Grand Prix 2025)

- **Calibrates**: Role, Big Idea.
- **Insight**: a price printed on the pack is a promise the brand physically cannot take back. Packaging lead times make it a commitment.
- **Why it wins**: inflation anxiety met by a medium that makes the promise unbreakable. The pack is the proof.
- **Operation**: Package as Medium (below).

#### Renault — "Plug-Inn" (Publicis Conseil, Paris · Creative Strategy Grand Prix 2023)

- **Calibrates**: Role, Creative.
- **Tension**: electric cars are useless in the countryside without chargers, and the grid will not arrive in time.
- **Role**: turn rural homeowners into the charging network. An app lets drivers plug in at private houses.
- **Why it wins**: the brand solves the category's structural problem instead of advertising around it.
- **Operation**: Build a Utility.

#### CeraVe — "Michael CeraVe" (Ogilvy PR, New York · Social & Influencer Grand Prix 2024)

- **Calibrates**: Small Ideas, participation.
- **Creative mechanic**: seed a conspiracy that actor Michael Cera is behind the skincare brand, let the internet investigate, then reveal at the Super Bowl.
- **Why it wins**: the audience does the amplification before the ad exists. The reveal pays off weeks of participation.
- **Operation**: Immersive Prank.

#### Dove — "Real Beauty Redefined for the AI Era" (Ogilvy, Mindshare, Droga5 · Media Grand Prix 2025)

- **Calibrates**: Role, Zeitgeist.
- **Zeitgeist**: generative AI is re-narrowing what "beautiful" looks like, twenty years after Real Beauty widened it.
- **Role**: train the algorithm instead of complaining about it. Real-beauty images uploaded to Pinterest reshape what its search returns.
- **Why it wins**: a two-decade platform finds its next enemy and acts on infrastructure, not sentiment.
- **Operation**: Infrastructure Correction.

### SEVEN OPERATIONS THE WINNERS SHARE

Reproducible moves drawn from the set above. They sit beside the catalog clusters; most combine a cluster technique with a proof mechanic.

1. **Bait-and-Switch** — lead with the visual the audience expects, then reveal the truth that exposes their assumption. Works when the bias is the real subject. *Orange.*
2. **Build a Utility** — do not make an ad; make the functional thing that proves the promise. The product of the campaign is a service, a job, a network. *Xbox, Renault.*
3. **Cultural Trojan Horse** — carry a heavy message inside a joke, song or meme the culture already owns. The vehicle does the persuading. *Specsavers.*
4. **Immersive Prank** — plant a mystery the audience wants to solve, let participation build, reveal last. *CeraVe.*
5. **Package as Medium** — use the physical product or pack to make a promise that cannot be undone. *Penny.*
6. **Aesthetic Authority** — elevate ordinary user output to gallery standard so the audience's own work becomes the proof. *Apple "Shot on iPhone", Outdoor Grand Prix 2015.*
7. **Infrastructure Correction** — change the system (an algorithm, a policy clause, a rulebook) rather than the message about it. *Dove, AXA.*

Tag the operation when it is in play, the same way a Creative Move is named. If a Big Idea uses none of these and none of the catalog techniques, ask whether it is an idea or a statement.
