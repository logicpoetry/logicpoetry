## BRIEF_TYPES (Where Logic Poetry Sits)

Not all briefs are the same. Understanding the type of brief determines how Logic Poetry should be applied. Three brief types exist in a solar system relationship:

**Brand Idea Brief (The Sun)**
All campaigns orbit around it. Used when the brief is about defining or repositioning the brand's core idea platform — the central truth everything else revolves around. Examples: Adidas "Impossible is Nothing," Apple "Think Different." Deliverables: brand name, tagline, idea platform, tone of voice, visual brand guide.

The Logic Poetry at this level: Run the full 9-step framework to generate the brand's foundational creative platform. This platform should be durable enough to house multiple campaigns over years.

**Campaign Idea Brief (The Planet)**
Orbits the brand idea. Used for quarterly or major campaign projects — integrated campaigns with multiple touchpoints. The campaign builds on the brand idea platform but has its own specific tension, insight, and creative execution. Example: Adidas "Beyond the Surface" (promoting inclusive swimwear in the Middle East — orbits "Impossible is Nothing" without replacing it).

The Logic Poetry at this level: This is the primary use case. Run the full 9-step framework. The Zeitgeist and Tension are campaign-specific, but the Role should connect back to the brand idea platform.

**Tactical Idea Brief (The Shooting Star)**
A one-off execution that supports the brand idea but doesn't require a full campaign. Used for weekly or short-term projects that fill gaps. Example: Adidas YouTube video on upcycling packaging for eco-friendly gift wrapping during holidays.

The Logic Poetry at this level: May not require the full 9 steps. Steps 1-3 (Zeitgeist, Tension, Insight) may be inherited from the parent campaign. Focus on Steps 7-9 (Creative Platform, Big Idea, Small Ideas) to generate the tactical execution. The GWTB checkpoint may be simplified or skipped if the parent campaign's strategy is already validated.

**Key principle**: A marketing brief (from the client) is not the same as a creative brief (from the agency strategist). The marketing brief provides the business perspective — goals, problems, budget, timings. The creative brief translates this into the consumer's perspective. Logic Poetry operates on the creative brief side: it takes business inputs and transforms them into consumer-facing strategy and creative.

**Where Logic Poetry sits in the strategy landscape**: There are three strategy types that relate to each other:

- **Brand Strategy** — defines the brand's core identity, positioning, and long-term direction. Upstream of Logic Poetry.
- **Comms Strategy** — the bridge between brand strategy and creative strategy. It defines *what is said, when it's said, and why it's said*. It includes the consumer journey, comms framework (phases and barriers), campaign blueprint (timeline and budget), and campaign ecosystem (how all executions connect). Comms strategy is the comprehensive insurance policy for the idea.
- **Creative Strategy** — defines *how and where* the idea comes to life. This is the platform and execution layer — choosing the creative vehicle and optimizing it for each channel.

Logic Poetry primarily powers creative strategy (Steps 1-7 generate the platform and idea). Steps 8-9 (Big Idea, Small Ideas) overlap with comms strategy territory — orchestration, ecosystem mapping, and tactical channel optimization. For a full campaign, Nicolas Chan's Blueprint (`frameworks/14-blueprint.md`) supplies the journey and handoff method inside Step 9. Detailed media buying, delivery scheduling and execution management remain outside the creative method.
