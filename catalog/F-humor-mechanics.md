### CLUSTER F: HUMOR MECHANICS

**F1. Extreme Consequence** — Absurdly exaggerated result of using the product.
**F2. Absurd Alternative** — Ridiculous substitute highlights the real product's benefit.
**F3. Extreme Effort** — Ridiculous effort to get/provide the product conveys desirability.
**F4. Cute-Wrapped Mortality** — Grim consequence in a friendly aesthetic. "Dumb Ways to Die."
**F5. Absurd Spokes-Character Monologue** — Ridiculous consistent character becomes content factory. Old Spice, Dos Equis.
**F6. Product as Punchline** — Withhold product until last moment as emotional release.
**F7. Strategic Misdirection** — Elaborate false premise to earn attention. Tourism Australia "Dundee."
**F8. Charm Over Fear** — Humor in serious categories instead of statistics.

**F9. The Spoof / Parody Play**
Mimic the style, tone, or format of a recognizable cultural artifact and subvert it for the brand. Audience familiarity provides the setup; the brand's twist is the payoff.
- Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" (Wieden+Kennedy) — parodies luxury grooming tropes
- Dollar Shave Club "Our Blades Are F***ing Great" — parodies corporate brand videos
- Newcastle Brown Ale "If We Made It" (Droga5) — parodied the concept of making a Super Bowl ad without making one
- Source: Pricken's "Spoofs and Parodies" chapter.

**F10. The "What If It's True?" Move**
Introduce a deliberately absurd, contradictory, or taboo statement as a starting point, then build logical argument from it. Provocation earns attention through shock; resolution earns respect.
- Diesel "Be Stupid" — provocative counter-positioning against rational consumerism
- Patagonia "Don't Buy This Jacket" — anti-consumption on Black Friday
- Source: Edward de Bono's PO (Provocative Operation) from lateral thinking methodology. Pricken's "Provocation and Shock Tactics" chapter.

**F11. The Surreal Displacement**
Place the product into an entirely surreal, dream-like, or logically impossible world. Abandoning realism signals "this is an experience, not a sales pitch."
- Cadbury "Gorilla" (Fallon) — gorilla playing Phil Collins drums; no rational connection to chocolate
- Skittles "Taste the Rainbow" — consistently surreal executions
- Source: Pricken's "Absurd, Surreal, Bizarre" chapter.
