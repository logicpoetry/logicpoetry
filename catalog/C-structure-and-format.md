### CLUSTER C: STRUCTURE & FORMAT

Techniques that manipulate the structure, medium, or delivery channel.

**C1. The Artifact**
Make a functional thing — object, tool, experience — that embodies the brand, not a message about the brand.
- One-star cookbook — Deliveroo
- Keyboard app with forced pause — SKM
- Spotify Wrapped — data product IS the campaign

**C2. The Constraint as Canvas**
A limitation structures the entire execution. The constraint IS the creative.
- Cheetos dust prevents doing anything → campaign built on that physical constraint
- SKM pause app → limitation IS the product

**C3. The Setup & Payoff (Reveal Mechanic)**
Withhold information, set up an expectation, then deliver the unexpected.
- Redress — visitors don't know garments are preowned until they love them
- Heinz Ketchup Fraud — you think it's Heinz but it's not

**C4. The Scale Shift**
Take something tiny and make it the entire story.
- Pampers — baby's facial expression becomes the campaign
- Cheetos dust — trivial annoyance becomes philosophical statement

**C5. The Found Object**
Something already existing in the real world gets repurposed as campaign material.
- Heinz Ketchup Fraud — originated from a real Snapchat
- Spotify Wrapped — data that already existed

**C6. The Format Hack**
Use a media format in a way it was never intended.
- Liquid billboard you can swim in — Adidas "Beyond the Surface"

**C7. One-Image Mic Drop**
Single visual + minimal text forces instant inference.
- "FCK" print ad — KFC

**C8. Time-Lapse as Argument**
Let passage of time produce the proof. Time becomes the copy.
- Moldy Whopper — 35 days of mold IS the ad

**C9. Built-to-Be-Spoofed Template**
Create a strict repeatable template that invites imitation. Imitations spread the platform.
- "Priceless" — Mastercard ("the meme before there were memes")

**C10. The Subtraction**
Remove an essential element. The absence draws attention.
- Source: SIT/Goldenberg.

**C11. The Unification**
Recruit an existing resource in the environment to carry the message.
- Billboard using a streetlamp's shadow as part of the creative
- IBM "Smart Planet" posters — billboards that doubled as rain shelters or wheelchair ramps
- Source: SIT/Goldenberg.

**C12. The Perspective Swap**
Tell the story from an unexpected point of view — the product's, the competitor's, an animal's, a future generation's, a bystander's.
- IKEA "Lamp" (Crispin Porter + Bogusky / Spike Jonze) — told from a discarded lamp's POV, making viewers feel sorry for it before the narrator says "That is because you are crazy."
- Sandy Hook Promise "Back to School" — school supplies described cheerfully while violence unfolds
- Source: Pricken's "A Change of Perspective" chapter. One of the most frequently awarded structures at Cannes Lions Film.

**C13. The Alternative Use / Product Displacement**
Show the product used for something other than intended. The unexpected application reinforces core qualities through unfamiliar context.
- IKEA "Cook This Page" (Leo Burnett Toronto) — recipe instructions printed on parchment you cook with
- WD-40 — cataloging "2,000+ uses" beyond original purpose
- Source: Pricken's "Alternative Uses" chapter.

**C14. The Inside View / X-Ray Effect**
Show what's normally hidden — inside the product, inside the body, inside a process. Making the invisible visible creates understanding and fascination.
- Dyson vacuum transparent casing — literally showing the engineering
- Anti-smoking campaigns — tar accumulation inside lungs
- Source: Pricken's "Take a Look Inside" chapter.
